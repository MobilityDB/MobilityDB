Detailed Description

The centroid of a geometry, judged by its closed form.

The centroid is the mean of the highest-dimensional pieces of the geometry weighted by their measure, and which dimension that is, is decided exactly: a surface holds an area wherever a ring's shoelace sum is not zero, however small that area is. The cases:

  • a triangle of base 10 and height h holds an area for every h > 0, so its centroid is (5, h / 3), at heights far below any band on the area;
  • a ring whose points lie on one line holds no area, so its centroid is that of its outline;
  • a hole wound the same way as its shell is subtracted, being the second ring, never added for its winding: (16 * 2 - 1 * 1.5) / 15;
  • a square, whose centroid is its centre.

To compile and run:

gcc -Wall -g -I/usr/local/include -o centroid_test centroid_test.c \
-L/usr/local/lib -lmeos -lm
./centroid_test
