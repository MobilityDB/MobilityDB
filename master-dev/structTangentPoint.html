Detailed Description

A point where a common tangent of two circles touches one of them, which no double holds in general: what defines it, exactly.

With along nonzero, which two circles of one radius r alone carry, the point lies past that one by r along the line of the centres: Ck + r (along d + side rot(d)) / |d|, d = C2 - C1, a corner of the square cap of a buffer, where its offset meets the tangent across the end

With arc given, it is instead the point where the offset of that input arc ends: the arc's points fix its circle, of centre C, and the point lies on the circle of radius r1 about its start (k 0) or its end (k 1), on the line through C, away from C for side 1 and toward it for -1: the end of the arc of radius R + side r1 about C. No double holds it in general. The arc is held out of line, as an edge holds its tangent points, so that a point where a tangent touches a circle keeps its size
