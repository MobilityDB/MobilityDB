# The cell walks: the oracle and its acceptance set

[Back to the index](00-INDEX.md) · [The rule](RULE.md) · [The enumeration](ENUMERATION.md) · [Implementation plan](IMPLEMENTATION-PLAN.md)

This note holds row CW3: an oracle that shares no code with the walks, the acceptance set it
answers, and the answers themselves, to be reviewed before any walk changes. A walk is then
correct on a row when it states, for each cell, exactly the period the oracle states.

## What it covers

| Part | State |
|---|---|
| H3, planar model (`tgeompoint`) | built: the oracle, 454 rows, their answers and the refuting controls below |
| H3, geodetic model (`tgeogpoint`) | built: the oracle on decision D6, the 154 named rows, their answers and controls |
| S2, planar and geodetic | built: the oracles on decisions D6 and D7, 147 planar and 106 geodetic rows, their answers and controls |
| QUADBIN | through `spaceSplit` of the same trajectory over the Mercator tiles, row CW5 |

## The oracle of the planar H3 cells

`cgal_h3_planar` (in the oracle kit, `oracle-kit/home/cpwork/cw3/`) reads each cell from the H3
library alone and decides every question in exact rational arithmetic (`CGAL::Gmpq`) on the input
doubles, so no decision rounds:

- **The cell** is the straight-edged outline of [the rule](RULE.md): its vertices from the vertex
  index (`cellToVertexes`, `vertexToLatLng`, `radsToDegs`), and the extra vertex of an edge
  crossing a face of the icosahedron from the directed edge leaving the lower of its two cells
  (`directedEdgeToBoundary`), joined by straight edges in longitude and latitude. A ring crossing the
  antimeridian is read in the longitudes of its path, shifted by exact multiples of 360, so two cells
  sharing a vertex keep it bit for bit. The cell around a pole is the region between its ring and
  the pole.
- **The owner** of a position is the cell holding it: its interior and its north and west sides, by
  the exact sign of each edge ([the enumeration](ENUMERATION.md)).
- **The crossings** of a segment are the exact parameters where it meets an edge of the cells near
  it. The candidate cells are closed until every owner found has its neighbours among them, so no
  crossing is missed; near a pole, where a straight-edged cell spans many degrees of longitude, the
  owner of a position is searched ring by ring around the library's cell.
- **The answer:** an instant belongs to the cell holding its exact position; a cell's period runs
  between two crossings, each bound the truncation of the crossing time to the microsecond,
  inclusive where the cell holds that microsecond (decision D3); a cell crossed between two
  microseconds holds no instant and has no period. With `borderInc` false, a cell holding only the
  last instant is left out.
- **Self-check:** the oracle reports any position held by no cell or by two. On the 454 rows it
  reports none.

## The acceptance set

454 rows, generated by `gen_acceptance.py` (seed 20261111): the named rows below, one or more for
every refuting class the plan lists, and 300 random trips near a vertex at resolutions 3, 5, 7 and
9, with `borderInc` true or false, through the vertex at an instant or inside a segment, at speeds
from 7 microseconds to a minute a segment.

answers no control moves could not catch a walk that is wrong in that way.

The polar and unwrap controls also break the partition itself: the oracle reports 54 and 158
positions held by no cell or by two. The bulge rows are refuted by the library's own cell: a walk
on the curved cell of `latLngToCell` crosses at another instant, 0.4, 0.05 and 0.3 seconds from the
expected one, 5.3, 0.8 and 4.1 km along the trips. The manual's example of `h3Split` at
resolution 4 (`Point(-0.1 51.5)` to `Point(0.1 51.5)`) moves the same way: the straight edge is
crossed at longitude 0.0037797 at `2001-01-01 12:27:12.810291`, where the curved cell is crossed
at 0.004068.

## The oracles of the geodetic H3 cells and of the S2 cells

Both are in the oracle kit (`h3_geodetic_oracle.py`, `s2_oracle.py`), share no code with MEOS or with
the S2 library, and answer in the same form as the planar oracle.

- **Geodetic H3.** A position is its D6 vector, a vertex the D6 vector of its degrees from the vertex
  index, an edge the great-circle arc between two vertices. A trip moves along the great circle at a
  constant angular speed, as MEOS moves it (`interpolate_point4d_spheroid` on the sphere). Every
  crossing is an exact rational direction (the cross product of the planes of the trip and of the
  edge), so which cell holds it, and holds the open stretch between two crossings, is an exact sign;
  only its instant is transcendental, enclosed in rigorous intervals until its truncation to the
  microsecond is certain. A crossing exactly at a microsecond is decided exactly where it is
  structural (at an end of the segment, or at the midpoint of two ends of equal length); any other the
  oracle reports as undecided, and none occurs on these rows. **An H3 cell with an extra vertex can
  be non-convex on the sphere** (`8108bffffffffff`, at its extra vertex), so membership is a crossing
  number in the gnomonic frame of the cell, where great circles are lines and the coordinates are
  rational.
- **S2.** A cell is (face, level, i, j). Its corners are exact on the cube and read by decision D7:
  the geodetic corner is the cube point with `u` and `v` correctly rounded, the planar corner the
  correctly rounded longitude and latitude of the exact corner. The owner of a boundary point is
  decided by index: the low-i and low-j edges and the corner between them, the two cube corners no
  cell holds as its low corner going to the lowest face. On these rows the instant at (1, −1, 1) goes
  to a cell of face 0 and the one at (−1, 1, −1) to a cell of face 1. The cell identifiers follow the
  Hilbert curve of S2 and agree with `s2sphere` on 3000 random cells.
- **For review: a corner at a pole in the plane.** From level 1 the poles are corners of S2 cells,
  where the longitude is undefined. Both edges meeting there run along meridians (`u = 0` or `v = 0`
  on faces 2 and 5), so in the planar model the pole corner reads as two vertices on the pole
  latitude, at the longitudes of those two meridians, and a position on the pole latitude is the pole,
  held by the cell whose held corner it is. A control reading the pole corner as one vertex leaves 46
  positions held by no cell or by two.
- **Where a geodetic trip meets a boundary exactly.** Under D6 a position lies exactly on an S2
  boundary only where its coordinates are exact: the equator and the meridians 0, 90 and 180 (grid
  lines from level 1), the meridians 45 and 135 (cube edges) and the poles (corners from level 1). The
  geodetic S2 rows are built there.

changes answers MEOS gives.

## The two decisions the geodetic and S2 parts rest on

- **D6, a geodetic position** is the vector of the correctly rounded cos(lat)·cos(lon),
  cos(lat)·sin(lon) and sin(lat) of its input degrees, and every decision an exact sign on those
  doubles. The exact point of the sphere is set aside: its coordinates are transcendental, so a
  sign that is exactly zero, such as that of three points on one meridian or on the equator, cannot
  be told from a small one at any precision. The correctly rounded vector is the same on every
  platform, and the oracle computes it from this statement with MPFR, sharing no code with MEOS.
- **D7, the vertices of an S2 cell:** a corner is exact on the cube, its `u` and `v` rational; its
  longitude and latitude are the correctly rounded doubles of the exact corner's angles, and its
  geodetic vector the cube point with `u` and `v` correctly rounded.
- **D8, the vertices of an H3 cell:** the nearest doubles of the degrees of the real values H3's
  formula gives them. The check D6 and D7 raised was run: H3 4.2 and H3 4.4.1 place 388 of the
  6846 cells of the resolutions 0 to 2 differently, and neither is the exact value (each matches about
  a third of the 82800 coordinates, at most 6.75e-13 degrees away). For vertex 0 of
  `8104bffffffffff` (face 1, IJK (2, 10, 0)) the exact longitude 129.3842607622459730893… rounds to
  `0x1.02c4bdd39df71p+7`, which H3 4.4.1 returns and H3 4.2 misses by one unit in the last place, and
  the exact latitude 66.9491555537302878166… rounds to `0x1.0bcbef6ef85a8p+6`, which both miss by
  one. The oracles move onto these vertices (`h3_exact.py`, `h3_recipes.c` of the oracle kit); on
  the 41220 points the cells of the resolutions 0 to 2 share with a neighbour, D8 states every one
  with the same doubles from both sides.
